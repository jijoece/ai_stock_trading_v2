# Keep the dashboard home, gate the edge

The best free way to reach this dashboard from a phone without a Tailscale client is to leave it on the Mac and put **Cloudflare Tunnel with Cloudflare Access** in front of it. Restrict Access to your one email address and use email one-time PIN or Google login. Optionally add an in-app identity check as a second layer. The SQLite DB, the scheduler, the Robinhood OAuth tokens and the Alpaca keys all stay on the Mac. The only recurring cost is a domain on Cloudflare DNS. The runner-up is **ngrok Free with its OAuth action**, which needs no domain but caps traffic at 1 GB and 20,000 requests a month and shows a warning page before the app. The fallback is to keep **Tailscale Funnel** (or any tunnel without auth) and put **oauth2-proxy** on the Mac in front of Streamlit. Moving the app to free cloud hosting is the weakest fit. Hugging Face and Fly.io no longer offer usable free tiers for this. Streamlit Community Cloud allows only one private app. And every cloud host puts the broker secrets where its staff, its infrastructure and anyone with push access to the deploy repo can reach them. Streamlit's built-in `st.login` is useful only as a second layer: it gates the code that runs after the check, not the server's WebSocket, media or component endpoints. The dashboard is strictly read-only, which lowers the stakes, but a leaked token or rendered portfolio data is still a real loss.

## An edge login in front of the Mac protects data and tokens best

A tunnel connects outbound from the Mac, so nothing moves and no port opens. The design question is whether a real identity check runs before any request reaches Streamlit. **Cloudflare Tunnel plus Access** is the strongest free answer. The Zero Trust free plan covers up to **50 users at $0, with 24-hour log retention**. I could not render Cloudflare's own plan page, so these figures come from secondary sources ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/business-vpn/cloudflare-zero-trust/free-plan/)). The account limits are far beyond what one user needs: **500 Access applications, 1,000 tunnels and 50 identity providers** ([Cloudflare One account limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/account-limits/)). For auth, Access can send a **one-time PIN that expires 10 minutes after the request** to an allowed email, so no identity provider is needed. OTP can also run alongside Google or GitHub login ([Cloudflare One-time PIN](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/identity/one-time-pin/)). Setup on macOS is first-party: `cloudflared service install` registers a launch agent that runs at login, and `sudo cloudflared service install` registers a launch daemon that runs at boot ([Cloudflare: run as macOS service](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/connections/connect-networks/configure-tunnels/local-management/as-a-service/macos/)). The catch is that a named tunnel needs a domain on Cloudflare DNS. The free `trycloudflare.com` quick tunnels need no domain, but they get a **new hostname every run, have a 200 in-flight request cap, have no uptime guarantee and have no auth**. Cloudflare positions them for testing only ([Cloudflare Quick Tunnels](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/connections/connect-networks/do-more-with-tunnels/trycloudflare/)).

**ngrok Free** is the no-domain alternative. It gives one stable `*.ngrok-free.app` domain that survives agent restarts, and free endpoints have no timeout ([ngrok Free plan limits](https://ngrok.com/docs/pricing-limits/free-plan-limits/)). Its OAuth action supports Google, GitHub, Microsoft and others, including managed apps that need no client secret, and you can deny every email except yours with a policy expression ([ngrok OAuth action](https://ngrok.com/docs/traffic-policy/actions/oauth/)). The free plan includes **3 monthly active identities** ([ngrok pricing](https://ngrok.com/pricing)). The limits are tighter: **1 GB of data out and 20,000 HTTP requests a month**, and an interstitial warning page before browser traffic that you have to click through every 7 days ([ngrok Free plan limits](https://ngrok.com/docs/pricing-limits/free-plan-limits/)). Streamlit keeps a WebSocket open and pushes updates on every rerun, so heavy daily use could exhaust 1 GB. Measure that before you rely on it. There is also an unresolved question. The pricing page lists Traffic Policy units as bought with credit, so it is unclear whether a $0 Free account can run the OAuth policy at all ([ngrok pricing](https://ngrok.com/pricing)). Test it first. The cheapest fix is Hobbyist at **$8/month billed annually** ([ngrok pricing](https://ngrok.com/pricing)).

The other tunnels do not fit well. **Tailscale Funnel** is free on every plan and uses a stable `*.ts.net` name. Its relay **cannot decrypt traffic**, because TLS ends on the Mac, which no other option here offers. But Funnel is still in beta, has unpublished bandwidth limits, needs the open-source `tailscaled` variant on macOS rather than the App Store app, and cannot share a port with Serve ([Tailscale KB: Funnel](https://tailscale.com/kb/1223/funnel)). It also sends **no identity headers for public requests**, so every visitor is anonymous ([Tailscale KB: Serve](https://tailscale.com/kb/1312/serve)). Funnel therefore needs an auth layer on the Mac. Pinggy Free has a **60-minute tunnel timeout and a random URL on restart** ([pinggy.io](https://pinggy.io/)). localtunnel documents no access control at all ([GitHub localtunnel](https://github.com/localtunnel/localtunnel)). frp offers visitors only HTTP Basic auth, and by default `frpc` does not verify the server certificate ([GitHub frp](https://github.com/fatedier/frp)). **Pangolin** is a credible newcomer. Its free cloud tier allows 5 users and 15 public resources, includes custom domains, and supports PIN, passcode and email-OTP gates. External identity providers are not on the free tier ([Pangolin pricing](https://pangolin.net/pricing); [GitHub Pangolin](https://github.com/fosrl/pangolin)). Its self-hosted edition is free, but it needs a VM with a public IP. I could not read zrok's hosted limits or auth details, because its docs moved to NetFoundry and the pages returned no body text ([NetFoundry zrok](https://netfoundry.io/docs/zrok/concepts/sharing-public/)).

Edge auth has one privacy cost. **Cloudflare and ngrok must terminate TLS to check identity, so they can see rendered portfolio values in plaintext.** The Robinhood and Alpaca credentials still never leave the Mac, as long as the dashboard never renders them. Abuse history also matters for reliability. Attackers used TryCloudflare to deliver the AsyncRAT and Xworm remote-access trojans, including a July 2024 wave of more than 1,500 messages ([Proofpoint](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/threat-insight/threat-actor-abuses-cloudflare-tunnels-deliver-rats)). That is why shared tunnel domains get blocklisted on some networks, and a custom domain avoids it. Whatever tunnel you choose, **the Mac must stay awake**. The tunnel only stays reliable if both Streamlit and the connector run as launchd services and sleep is disabled.

## Free cloud hosting moves the secrets, and the free tiers have shrunk

Hosting the app itself means the SQLite DB, and possibly the broker credentials, have to leave the Mac. That is the core conflict with your constraints. The free options have also narrowed. **Hugging Face now requires PRO ($9/month) to create any Docker Space**, and Streamlit runs only as a Docker Space there ([HF Spaces overview](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview); [HF pricing](https://huggingface.co/pricing)). **Fly.io has no free tier for new organizations**, only a $1 credit for 7 days ([Fly pricing](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing)). The remaining free hosts break down as follows.

| Host | Free compute and limits | Persistent disk | Built-in viewer gate | Card needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Streamlit Community Cloud | 0.078–2 CPU, 690 MB–2.7 GB; sleeps after 12h idle ([docs](https://docs.streamlit.io/deploy/streamlit-community-cloud/manage-your-app)) | Rebuilt from repo | Email allowlist; **one private app only** ([docs](https://docs.streamlit.io/deploy/streamlit-community-cloud/share-your-app)) | No |
| Google Cloud Run + IAP | 180k vCPU-s, 360k GiB-s, 2M requests/month (search snippet of [pricing](https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing)) | Ephemeral | IAP Google-identity allowlist ([docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/securing/identity-aware-proxy-cloud-run)) | Not verified |
| Oracle Always Free | Ampere A1 equivalent to 2 OCPU / 12 GB; 10 TB egress ([docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)) | 200 GB block | None (self-managed VM) | Not verified |
| Render free | Spins down after 15 min; ~1 min cold start; 750 h/month ([docs](https://render.com/docs/free)) | None | None | No |
| Koyeb free | 512 MB, 0.1 vCPU; scales to zero after 1 h ([docs](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/reference/instances)) | 2 GB SSD | None | Yes ([FAQ](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/faqs/pricing)) |
| Railway Free | 0.5 GB RAM, $1/month credit, no custom domains ([pricing](https://railway.com/pricing)) | 500 MB volume | None | No |

Only three of these hosts gate viewers by identity before the app runs: **Streamlit Community Cloud, Hugging Face private Spaces (now paid) and Cloud Run with IAP**. IAP is the strongest. It covers every ingress path, including the default `run.app` URL, without a load balancer ([Cloud Run IAP](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/securing/identity-aware-proxy-cloud-run)). Community Cloud's allowlist has a weakness: invited viewers **"can invite others"**, so you are not the only one who controls access ([SCC: Share your app](https://docs.streamlit.io/deploy/streamlit-community-cloud/share-your-app)). Render, Koyeb, Railway and Oracle give you a public URL and leave auth to you.

The DB is solvable. Read-only pages tolerate an ephemeral disk if the app fetches a fresh snapshot. After each run, the Mac scheduler would write a consistent copy with SQLite's `.backup` command or `VACUUM INTO` and upload it to a private bucket, and the cloud app would download it on a short cache timer. **Cloudflare R2's free tier gives 10 GB of storage and charges no egress fees**, which covers this at $0 ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). Litestream cuts staleness to seconds by streaming changes to S3-compatible storage. It needs a long-running process on the Mac, and R2 is not listed by name in its config reference ([Litestream config](https://litestream.io/reference/config/)). Turso's free plan (100 databases, 5 GB, no card required) is another route, but it means rewriting the DB layer and storing the data with another vendor ([Turso pricing](https://turso.tech/pricing)). Pushing the DB to a private git repo works on Community Cloud, but it redeploys the app on every push and keeps all the research data in GitHub history.

The secrets cannot be fixed this way. On every platform, secrets reach the app as environment variables or `secrets.toml`, so any code in the app can read them ([HF Spaces overview](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview); [SCC secrets management](https://docs.streamlit.io/deploy/streamlit-community-cloud/deploy-your-app/secrets-management)). Anyone with push access to the deploy repo can therefore read them too. None of the fetched documentation describes whether platform staff can see secrets. There is also a functional problem. If a Robinhood refresh flow writes rotated tokens to disk, an ephemeral host loses them on restart. Community Cloud's IP addresses also change without notice ([SCC: Status and limitations](https://docs.streamlit.io/deploy/streamlit-community-cloud/status)). The only cloud design that meets your "no secrets to third parties" rule is to **host only the research pages with an R2 snapshot, and turn the Score a Ticker and Robinhood Portfolio pages off with a config flag**. That gives up the opt-in pages when you are away from home.

## Streamlit's own login gates code, not the server

Streamlit has had native OIDC login since **1.42**. You install `streamlit[auth]`, add an `[auth]` block with `redirect_uri`, `cookie_secret` and the provider credentials, and call `st.login()` ([st.login API](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/api-reference/user/st.login)). Turning it on forces CORS and XSRF protection on. Two-factor authentication comes from the identity provider, such as Google 2-Step Verification. The limitations matter for a single private user. Streamlit has **no built-in email allowlist**, so your code must compare `st.user.email` against your address on every page. The identity cookie **lasts 30 days, and you cannot change that or tie it to the token's expiry**. Logging out in one tab does not log out other sessions ([Streamlit authentication](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/concepts/connections/authentication)). It also needs a stable HTTPS URL for the redirect, so tunnels with random URLs do not work with it.

The bigger issue is coverage. Reading the installed **Streamlit 1.59.2** source shows that the `/_stcore/stream` WebSocket accepts any connection from an allowed Origin. It reads the auth cookie only to fill in `st.user` and starts a script session either way. Routes such as `/media/*`, `/component/*`, `/_stcore/health` and the frontend bundle do no login check. So an unauthenticated visitor runs your script's imports and top-level code before reaching `st.stop()`, and can identify the Streamlit version. This is not hypothetical. **CVE-2026-33682**, an unauthenticated SSRF in the component endpoints fixed in 1.54.0, was reachable without login. It affected only Windows hosts ([GitHub advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-7p48-42j8-8846)). The third-party streamlit-authenticator package has the same coverage gap and weaker identity. It has had no release since **0.4.2 in March 2025**, and its 2FA feature is marked "phased out" ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/streamlit-authenticator/); [GitHub](https://github.com/mkhorasani/Streamlit-Authenticator)).

A reverse proxy on the Mac closes that gap, because Streamlit then sees only traffic that has already been authenticated. **oauth2-proxy** is the lightest option. It is one binary that proxies WebSockets by default, listens on `127.0.0.1:4180`, sets a 168-hour secure cookie, uses Google by default, and restricts access to the emails listed in `--authenticated-emails-file`. It is a one-line allowlist ([oauth2-proxy configuration](https://oauth2-proxy.github.io/oauth2-proxy/configuration/overview)). Authelia behind Caddy adds self-hosted TOTP and WebAuthn, but it needs a user database and a separate proxy ([Authelia Caddy integration](https://www.authelia.com/integration/proxies/caddy/)). Pomerium turns WebSockets off by default, so each route needs `allow_websockets: true` ([Pomerium timeouts](https://www.pomerium.com/docs/reference/routes/timeouts)). Caddy's `basic_auth` uses bcrypt hashes and gates every path, but it offers no 2FA and is safe only over HTTPS ([Caddy basic_auth](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/directives/basic_auth)). There is one pitfall. If you pass identity to Streamlit through the hidden, experimental `server.trustedUserHeaders` option, anyone who reaches port 8501 directly can forge it. Keep Streamlit bound to 127.0.0.1, as it is now. For hardening, keep CORS and XSRF on, keep static serving off, and set `client.showErrorDetails="none"` and `browser.gatherUsageStats=false` ([config.toml docs](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/api-reference/configuration/config.toml)).

## Ranked recommendation for this dashboard

| Rank | Option | Cost | Domain | Data and tokens on Mac | Effort | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cloudflare Tunnel + Access (one-email OTP or Google), plus an optional in-app email check | $0 plus a domain | Required | Yes; Cloudflare sees rendered pages | Low–medium | Mac must stay awake; Cloudflare sees plaintext |
| 2 | ngrok Free + OAuth (deny all but your email) | $0, or $8/month | No (`*.ngrok-free.app`) | Yes; ngrok sees rendered pages | Low | 1 GB/20k-request caps; interstitial; Free OAuth eligibility unverified |
| 3 | Tailscale Funnel → oauth2-proxy → Streamlit | $0 | No (`*.ts.net`) | Yes; relay cannot decrypt | Medium | Funnel is beta; you run the auth layer yourself |
| 4 | Pangolin (Cloud Free or self-hosted) | $0 (VM if self-hosted) | Included or your own | Yes; Pangolin or your VM sees rendered pages | Medium–high | Younger project; no external IdP on the free cloud tier |
| 5 | Community Cloud or Cloud Run + IAP, research pages only, R2 snapshot | $0 | No | **DB snapshot leaves the Mac**; secrets stay home only if broker pages are off | Medium–high | Stale data; opt-in pages unavailable; SCC viewers can re-invite |
| Not advised | Quick tunnels, Pinggy Free, localtunnel, bare frp, or `st.login` alone on a public URL | $0 | — | Yes | Low | No real perimeter; ephemeral URLs |

Choose option 1 if you are willing to buy a domain. Choose option 2 if you are not, and test its OAuth policy and bandwidth first. Choose option 3 if you want to keep everything on infrastructure you already run and never let a provider see the decrypted content.

## Conclusion

The real constraint here is the broker secrets, not compute. They rule out cloud hosting much more firmly than the shrinking free tiers do. Once the Mac stays the only place secrets live, the decision comes down to who checks identity and who sees plaintext. Cloudflare and ngrok check identity at their edge in exchange for seeing the rendered pages. The Funnel-plus-oauth2-proxy stack keeps plaintext on the Mac in exchange for running your own auth. In every case, `st.login` adds little on its own but is a cheap second layer. Its real value is turning a misconfigured tunnel into a login screen instead of an open dashboard.

Several points remain unverified and are worth a 30-minute test before you commit. First, confirm that Cloudflare Access OTP and Google login are free-plan features; I could not read that on a Cloudflare primary page. Second, check whether ngrok Free with $0 credit can run an OAuth policy. Third, confirm WebSocket behavior and idle timeouts for Streamlit through each tunnel, since no provider page I read confirmed it. Finally, check what scope Robinhood's OAuth tokens carry, which I did not research. The dashboard's calls are read-only, but the token's scope decides what a leak would actually expose.
